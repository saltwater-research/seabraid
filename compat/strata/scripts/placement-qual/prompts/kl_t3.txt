The committee met on the third floor to decide how the old library should be used. One member wanted reading rooms with long tables and green lamps. Another argued for a workshop where students could build small machines, and a third wanted only an empty hall where people could gather without any particular purpose. The chairman listened to each in turn, writing three words on a pad: quiet, making, meeting. He pointed out that the building had large windows on the south side, a basement with thick walls, and a roof that leaked in only two places. After an hour they agreed on a compromise. The ground floor would stay open and loud, the upper floors would be silent, and the basement would hold the workbenches and the tools. A budget was drawn up on the back of an envelope, and each member promised to find one donor before the end of the month. When the meeting ended, the rain had stopped, and the light on the stairs made the dust look like fine gold.
